# English Writing Guide

English is the source language. Every other language is translated from it.

## Names

- Use Title Case. Capitalize every word except a, an, and, as, at, but, by, for, from, in, into, nor, of, on, onto, or, the, to, vs and with, unless the word comes first or last. Capitalize each part of a hyphenated word except those small words: Push-Up, Bent-Over Row, Behind-the-Neck Press.
- Put the equipment first, then the position or variation, then the movement: Dumbbell Incline Bench Press, Cable Seated Row, Barbell Romanian Deadlift, Dumbbell Single-Arm Row.
- Leave the equipment out for body-weight exercises: Push-Up, Pull-Up, Plank. Add Bodyweight only when the name alone would be unclear, as in Bodyweight Squat.
- Leave the equipment out when the usual name already means one machine: Leg Press, Leg Extension, Lying Leg Curl, Lat Pulldown, Hack Squat, Pec Deck Fly.
- Hyphenate compound modifiers that come before the movement: Behind-the-Neck Press, Close-Grip Bench Press, Single-Arm Row.
- Use these words: Single-Arm and Single-Leg (not One-Arm), Alternating, Close-Grip, Wide-Grip, Reverse-Grip, Neutral-Grip, Incline, Decline, Seated, Standing, Lying, Kneeling.
- Use these spellings: Push-Up, Pull-Up, Chin-Up, Sit-Up, Step-Up, Get-Up, Fly, Pulldown, Pushdown, Deadlift, Kickback, Rollout. Use American spelling.
- Use the singular: Calf Raise, not Calf Raises.
- No brackets, version numbers, camera views or demonstrator gender in names.

## Aliases

- Other names people really use for the same exercise: Bench Press, Press-Up, RDL.
- Not misspellings, and never the name or alias of another exercise.

## Steps

- Three to six steps. Each is one full sentence in the imperative, ending with a period.
- The first step sets up the position, grip and stance. The middle steps describe the movement. The last step returns to the start or finishes the exercise.
- No sets, reps, weights or times. Angles are fine.
- For one-side exercises, say once when to switch sides.
- Write in your own words. Never copy text from another source.

## Tips

- One to three short cues about the most important form points or the most common mistakes. Each is one full sentence.
